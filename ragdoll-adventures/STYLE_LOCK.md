# Approved style and storytelling lock — version 1

Owner correction recorded 26 October 2026. This contract takes precedence over generic descriptive style prompts and all rejected experiments.

## Reference and scope

**The Leaf Boat - Animated Garden Test** is the approved reference for animation, character finish, colour/lighting treatment and the use of multiple scenes to tell a visual story. Its geography and individual actions still require correction; approval of its look does not certify its continuity or final edit.

**The Leaf Boat - Continuity Revision** is rejected. Do not use its master image, more realistic environment, rugged dolls, repeated fixed composition or static calm-water rewrite as references. Earlier photographic tests are historical. A banner is branding, never a production reference.

Before any generation, open the approved test in the owner's account. Compare representative wide, medium and detail frames. Record the exact private media identity and timecodes in the run record, with observations of the animation and character finish. If the reference cannot be inspected, finish writing/planning but hold generation. Do not guess the look from the words "cinematic 3D". Keep reference images/IDs private.

## What must match

- Clearly animated, colourful storybook scenery with soft stylised forms, matching the approved test's palette, materials and lighting treatment. No drift toward photographic real-world scenery.
- Soft, appealing animated interpretations of the original crochet siblings. Retain recognisable yarn hair, embroidered blue eyes, clothing, colours and proportions; use the approved test's softened yarn/material finish. Do not maximise rough fibres, distress, dirt, coarse pores or gritty photorealism.
- Sister's brown pigtails/purple ties and pink dress; brother's upright brown hair, turquoise/white top and turquoise trousers. Compare both against the photographs for identity AND the approved animation for finish. Neither reference replaces the other.
- Warm sibling dialogue with physical actions, expressive reactions and humour. No narrator.

## Visual storytelling and camera

A continuous location can contain a path, bank, obstacle and destination. Establish their connection in a master layout, then use wide shots, mediums, inserts and reaction shots as the story requires. Keep a readable screen direction and geography. A fixed camera applies only within a particular shot when needed; never impose it on the whole episode.

Every shot must add a visible story event or a motivated emotional payoff. Record what changes physically from start to end. A character saying something happened is not evidence that it happened. A reaction beat may be still briefly when the timing is deliberate; it cannot replace all physical story progression.

For the 35-second proof, plan at least three distinct, purposeful compositions and at least two visible cause-and-effect actions. This is a minimum proof requirement, not permission for random angle changes or padding. Repeated composition is fine when motivated, but five dialogue shots at the same spot fail the test.

## Acceptance and change control

Compare each keyframe and rendered clip side by side with the approved animation. Reject style/material drift before generating dependent shots. Record reviewer, observation and private evidence for style, character finish, story/action agreement, continuity, pacing and audio.

A render failure does not reopen the style contract. Fix the shot's references, staging, prompt or cut; split the action if necessary while retaining its causal outcome. Do not replace sailing with sitting still, omit a promised action, simplify away the story, or change the visual style to make a failed generation acceptable. After repeated failure, mark the run blocked/incomplete and explain the specific problem. A material story or style change requires the owner's direction; routine shot repairs within the locked intent do not need another approval.
