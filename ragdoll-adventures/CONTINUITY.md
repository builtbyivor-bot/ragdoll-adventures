# Required scene, action and animation checks

These are acceptance gates for every episode, including revisions. A plausible prompt or good still image does not prove a correct video.

## Style, visual story and pacing are also mandatory

Read [STYLE_LOCK.md](STYLE_LOCK.md) and [FACTORY.md](FACTORY.md). Preserve The Leaf Boat - Animated Garden Test's approved animation and soft character finish. The rejected Continuity Revision is not a reference. Do not solve geography by freezing the camera/characters across the film. Use purposeful varied shots within the same mapped world. Every shot must add visible action or a motivated reaction; dialogue alone is insufficient. Reject rugged/photorealistic drift, unexplained quiet holds and padding. Failed required motion stays failed; do not rewrite the story to excuse it.

## Before generation

1. Lock a location ID and type, environment references, layout, landmarks, scale, lighting/weather and camera axis. Map the route of the story's action.
2. Lock prop IDs, count, appearance, size, ownership and state; track each doll's position, pose, hands, feet and wet/dry state.
3. Write each shot's exact start → action → end, with timing relative to dialogue.
4. Derive visible pass/fail tests from the story: what must move, remain stationary, be touched, enter/leave frame or arrive at a destination?
5. Match the next shot's starting state to the preceding accepted ending state. Plan any relocation, retrieval or time passage explicitly.

## While reviewing each finished clip

Watch the entire motion with sound, after final voice processing.

| Story claim | Required visible evidence |
|---|---|
| Moving / sailing / drifting | Object changes position relative to fixed scenery in the intended direction. Camera motion or bobbing alone is insufficient. |
| Still / stuck / stopped | Object stays at the same location relative to fixed scenery for that beat. Background ripples may move without moving the stuck object. |
| Around a stone | Continuous recognisable route passes around the same stone; no teleport, replacement obstacle or reset. |
| Into a destination | Object visibly arrives in the established destination connected to its route. |
| Put down / release / pick up | The named character visibly performs the transfer; hands and prop state agree before and after. |
| Dry feet / socks | Visible feet remain out of water and dry throughout the relevant action. |
| Same river in consecutive scenes | Same waterway type, banks, landmarks, depth/scale and flow direction; no substituted pond, puddle, basin or unrelated stream. |

Check exact speech, speaker identity, timing, intelligibility, lip-sync and listener silence alongside the action. A spoken claim must agree with what is visibly happening at that moment.

## At every cut

Compare the prior ending and next starting frames, then watch the join in motion:
- Same location and connected geography; consistent screen direction and camera axis.
- Same dolls, wardrobe, proportions, scale and light/weather.
- Same unique prop, shape, count, position, orientation, owner and motion state.
- No unexplained movement between banks, reversal, duplication, disappearance, waterway substitution, wet/dry reset or sudden retrieval.
- The new shot continues the actual accepted animation, not merely the original prompt's intended ending.

For a leaf-boat story, a leaf floating in a river cannot reappear in a pond or on dry stone without an established route or shown retrieval. Design the story to stay within one setting when a transition adds no useful story beat.

## Failure and handover

Record clip ID, expected evidence, actual observation, pass/fail and correction. Reject/regenerate or redesign the affected shot, then recheck adjacent shots and the assembled sequence. Do not repair missing motion by changing only the written description or by claiming it happened off screen.

Complete editing, voice work, timing, joins and all available internal reviews before owner handover. If a required check is impossible, disclose it and label the draft unverified/incomplete rather than claiming readiness. Never use owner review as a substitute for internal quality control.

Save and reopen the editable project to verify its clips. Present numbered clips/time ranges. An internal VideoExpress export may be used for review if buffering makes the timeline impractical. The owner must explicitly give final 100% approval before any final video download to the PC.
